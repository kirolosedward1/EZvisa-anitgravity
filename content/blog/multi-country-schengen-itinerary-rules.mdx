One of the greatest benefits of the Schengen visa is borderless travel. Once you enter the Schengen Area, you can take a train from France to Germany, or fly from Italy to Spain, without ever showing your passport.

However, when *applying* for a visa for a multi-country trip from the UAE, applicants frequently make a critical error: **They apply to the wrong embassy.**

Applying to the wrong embassy results in an immediate rejection for "lack of jurisdiction." Here are the exact rules you must follow.

## The "Main Destination" Rule (The Primary Rule)

Schengen law dictates that you must apply to the consulate of the country where you will spend the **majority of your nights.**

**Example 1:**
* You fly into Paris, France (stay 2 nights).
* Take a train to Rome, Italy (stay 6 nights).
* Fly back to Dubai from Rome.

*Result:* You must apply to the **Italian** embassy, because you are spending more nights there, even though France is your first point of entry.

## The "First Entry" Rule (The Tie-Breaker)

What happens if you are doing a road trip and spending an equal number of nights in different countries? That is when the "first entry" rule takes over.

**Example 2:**
* You fly into Munich, Germany (stay 4 nights).
* Drive to Vienna, Austria (stay 4 nights).
* Drive to Zurich, Switzerland (stay 4 nights).

*Result:* Because there is no single main destination (it’s a 3-way tie), you must apply to the embassy of the country you step foot in first. In this case, **Germany**.

## Why Embassies Reject Itineraries

Consular officers will count the nights on the hotel bookings you submit. If they calculate that you are spending 5 nights in Spain and 6 nights in Portugal, the Spanish embassy will refuse to process your application and send you away, forcing you to start the appointment booking process all over again for Portugal.

Furthermore, if your itinerary looks artificially balanced just so you can apply to an "easy" embassy (e.g., claiming 4 nights in Greece and 3 in Germany when you actually intend to spend 7 days in Germany), the officer may suspect "visa shopping" and reject the application entirely.
